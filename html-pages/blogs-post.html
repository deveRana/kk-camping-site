10 things to do at Pawna Lake
(that aren't just camping)

AD
Ananya Deshpande
Co-founder, Lakeora · 14 Sept 2026 · 8 min read

Ask most people what to do at Pawna Lake and they'll say the same thing: camp, bonfire, sleep, leave. It's a fine weekend. But we've spent enough weekends here to know the lake has more layers than that.

Here's our field guide — the corners of Pawna we send friends to, in the order we send them.

1. Watch the sunrise from Tikona Fort

Start at 5:30 AM. It's a 45-minute drive from the lake plus a 45-minute walk up. By 7 AM you're standing on a 3,000-ft ridge with Pawna glinting like glass below you and the Sahyadris rolling off in every direction. Cold chai from your flask hits different up here.

Pro tip: Wear proper shoes, not slippers. The last 100 metres is a bit of a scramble.

2. Kayak the lake at sunrise

The water is glass-flat before 7 AM. Rent a solo kayak (₹500) at any of the ghats and paddle out. Look back at the shore lit orange, mist coming off the water. This is what people mean when they say "peaceful."

3. Find the tiny chai stall at Kadadhe village

Not on Google Maps. Ask any local for "Baburao ki tapri." It's a 3-table setup outside a house that's been serving cutting chai and kanda bhaji for 30 years. ₹15 chai, ₹40 bhaji plate. Best after the trek.

"You haven't done Pawna until you've had chai at Baburao's, watched a cricket match on his tiny TV, and been told which route to take back."

4. Try local Malvani food, not Punjabi

Every camp serves the same butter chicken and paneer tikka. Ask your host if they can arrange ghawan (rice pancake) with sol kadhi and a coconut chicken curry. Different world. Costs about the same.

5. Bonfire yes, but also stargazing

After the bonfire dies down (usually 11 PM), walk 100 metres from the tents so your eyes can adjust. On a clear night you'll see the Milky Way. Actually see it. Download the SkyView app before you come — no signal at the lake but the app works offline.

6. Cycle around the eastern shore

Ask your host for a cycle (usually ₹200/day). The eastern shore road is unpaved but flat — a 90-minute loop past small farms, buffalo herds, and three temples nobody visits.

7. Visit the Lohagad Fort caves

Lohagad is 30 minutes away. Everyone climbs to the top. But the real experience is the caves cut into the cliff face 300 metres before the summit — Buddhist caves from the 1st century BCE, mostly empty on weekdays. Bring a torch.

8. Try boat fishing with local fishermen

Not touristy, not organized — just walk down to the eastern shore around 5 AM and ask a fisherman if you can join. ₹500 usually gets you in the boat for two hours. You probably won't catch anything, but you'll see the lake from the middle at first light.

9. Rain trek to Kondeshwar waterfall (monsoon only)

Only Jul–Sept. Short 40-minute hike through paddy fields to a 60-ft waterfall you can stand under. Slippery — go with someone local. This one is our secret and we're trusting you with it.

10. Do absolutely nothing

Take a book, a hammock, a bar of dark chocolate. Find a spot 20 metres from your tent. Turn your phone off. Stay there until you get bored, then stay another hour.

This is what Pawna is really for.

Ananya is a co-founder of Lakeora. She's spent 41 weekends at Pawna Lake and counting. She'd like to note that "Baburao ki tapri" is real but she'll deny it if you tell anyone.
